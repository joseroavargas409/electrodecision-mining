# Mining electrification: protect production while planning charging

**User:** mining planners and engineering consultants reviewing a battery-electric fleet or its charging infrastructure.

**Problem:** daily energy estimates can conceal simultaneous power demand, charger queues and a battery deficit carried into the next shift. Project teams also need to identify missing duty-cycle, electrical, compatibility and safety evidence before committing to equipment and works.

**Solution:** a reproducible synthetic fleet simulator compares four charging alternatives, carries per-vehicle energy into a second shift and exposes production, waiting, overload and inventory. An evidence workflow adapts review actions to technology, stage and jurisdiction.

**Role of AI:** NVIDIA Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory calls read-only calculation/context tools. For project reports, the model selects evidence IDs; the application validates them and renders catalog-backed statements and deterministic counts.

**Demonstration:** the same synthetic 1,700-t target yields 1,188 t with one charger, 1,404 t with two on the same supply, and 1,782 t with two plus 350 kW. Unmanaged charging reaches 1,782 t but exceeds supply for 369 minutes. The upgraded case yields 1,872 t in shift two after a 60-minute break; the horizon is 25 hours.

**Distinctive combination:** production-aware charging comparison, explicit shift-to-shift battery inventory and inspectable evidence-constrained AI review. This combines existing engineering and AI methods; no claim of a first-ever invention is made.

**Evidence and next step:** public MIT source, automated tests and a saved real model/tool trace. Calibrate with metered mine data, equipment behavior and planner feedback before using results for investment or engineering decisions.
